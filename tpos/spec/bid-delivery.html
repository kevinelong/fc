1 · How to Use This Pack — the Four Levers

The Product Specification describes the full product; a bid priced against "all of it, at once" is neither costable nor advisable. This pack cuts the work into priceable shape. Four decisions — the levers — swing the price more than everything else combined, and they are the client's calls:

LeverProposed defaultBid impact if changed
Scope cutlinePhase-1 MVP only (§2)Pricing all phases at once multiplies the bid 2–3×
Delivery modelHosted multi-tenant only; self-host deferredAdding self-host ≈ +30–50% (dual release/test/support)
Design ownershipBidder produces visual design from the Spec's IA wireframesClient-supplied hi-fi designs remove a whole phase
Launch jurisdictionUS, one-to-few states, USD, EnglishMulti-state tax/title + i18n are sizeable adders

Everything downstream — the MoSCoW tags (§2), the vendor register (§3), the assumptions (§4), the effort ranges (§5) and the stage gates (§6) — tightens dramatically once these four are fixed. Read with: ① the Product Specification (the requirements being priced) and ② the Functional Specification Proposal (the proposed technical solutions the estimate assumes).

2 · MVP Scope Matrix & Release Roadmap

The specification describes the full product. To bid accurately, the work must be cut into releases — otherwise a bidder prices “everything at once,” which is neither costable nor advisable. This appendix proposes a Phase-1 (MVP) cutline and tags every capability MoSCoW. PROPOSAL The cutline is the client’s to confirm; it is the single largest driver of the bid.

Proposed three-phase roadmap. Phase 1 delivers the core sell-loop as a hosted product; Phase 2 adds per-role depth; Phase 3 opens new markets and delivery models. Self-host, the platform-admin billing plane, additional accounting systems and a mobile field app are deliberately deferred — each is a major cost lever that should not inflate the first bid.

6 · Delivery Stages (Internal Demo → GA)

A maturity ladder, orthogonal to the feature-depth roadmap (§2): each stage takes a slice of the Phase-1 scope and raises the bar on what is real — data, persistence, security, money, SLA. The decisive boundary is between B and C. Stages A–B are facade/prototype-grade (and A is largely already built); C–E are the real engineering priced in §5. The governing rule: data sensitivity gates the quality bar, not the calendar.

Five stage-gates from internal demo to general availability. The dashed line marks the facade→real-build boundary: A–B are a few person-weeks of polish on the existing prototype; C–E are essentially the entire §5 estimate. Tenant isolation becomes mandatory at C (real data); the legally-required pen test and GLBA Safeguards baseline land at D (real PII + money); SLA, billing and support gate E.

6.1 Effort & the two principles

  • A/B are facade; C/D/E are the build. A is essentially done; B is ~1–3 pw of polish on the existing prototype. C–E together are the full ≈95–175 pw §5 estimate, split roughly: C ≈ foundation + core MUST roles (~40–60% of MVP), D ≈ the remainder + security/QA/pen-test packages, E ≈ the UAT/hardening/launch-readiness tail + billing/support setup. Do not let the polished sales demo imply the system is “almost done.”
  • Data sensitivity gates the bar, not time. Tenant isolation is required the moment real data exists (C). Real PII and money — and therefore the legally-required pen test and GLBA Safeguards baseline (Spec App. B) — only enter at beta (D), the stage not to rush. SLA, runbooks, billing and support gate GA (E).

Cross-references: stage scope maps to the MoSCoW tags and roadmap in §2; integration availability per stage to §3 (e.g., QuickBooks file export at D, QBO API in Phase 2/post-GA; soft-credit-pull stubbed until financing); security gates to Spec App. B; effort to §5.
