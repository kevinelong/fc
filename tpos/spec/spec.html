1 · Product Vision & Business Model

TPOS is a vertical SaaS that gives an independent trailer dealership one system to run both its public storefront and its entire back office — acquisition, reconditioning, pricing, marketing, sales, financing, titling, delivery and bookkeeping — on a single shared record of truth.

The market is fragmented small-to-mid dealers (used & refurbished commercial trailers: dry vans, flatbeds, reefers, lowboys, dumps, tankers, chassis) who today stitch together a website builder, a generic CRM, spreadsheets, QuickBooks and listing sites (TruckPaper, Facebook). TPOS replaces that stack with a purpose-built, role-aware platform.

The platform operator (TPOS, Inc.) additionally runs a super-admin control plane to provision tenants, meter usage, bill subscriptions, and support dealers (see §6.12).

Product principles

  • One record, many lenses. A trailer is simultaneously inventory, a deal, a work order, an invoice line, a title file, a delivery, and a public listing. Every role edits the same entity through a view shaped to its job — never a copy.
  • The storefront is generated, not separate. Inventory marked available is automatically published to the dealer's branded public site and syndicated to listing channels.
  • Role-first, not module-first. Each user lands on a workspace built around their daily goals and decisions, not a generic menu of tables.
  • Auditable by default. Price changes, discounts, status transitions and money movement are logged with who/when/why.

2 · Multi-Tenancy & Deployment Modes

Each dealership is a tenant with isolated data, its own users, branding, lots, inventory and storefront domain. The system must support three deployment modes from one codebase:

Hosted (SaaS)Operator-run cloudMany tenants per deployment (logical isolation, per-tenant scoping on every query)Recurring subscription, usage-metered
Licensed self-hostDealer infrastructureTypically single tenant; multi-tenant allowedAnnual license key, update channel

Requirement. Tenant isolation must be enforced at the data layer (no cross-tenant read/write possible), branding/config is per-tenant, and a tenant's storefront resolves on a custom domain or subdomain. The control plane (§6.12) exists only in hosted mode.

The data model is small and tightly linked. The Trailer (Inventory Unit) is the spine; everything references it.

Entity-relationship overview. One trailer threads through inventory, deals, work orders, invoices, title and delivery; each role reads the same graph.

The status machine

An Inventory Unit moves through: incoming → recon → available → pending → sold. Status is the single field that re-shapes the unit across roles — e.g. available publishes it to the storefront; pending hides it from new leads; sold moves it to delivery & books revenue. The system must guard valid transitions and log each one.

7 · Public Storefront

Every tenant gets a branded, mobile-first public website auto-generated from its available inventory. It is the demand engine that feeds the Lead Desk.

Functional requirements

  • Inventory grid with filter (type, condition, status), keyword search and sort (featured / price / year); each unit shows photos, key specs, price, location and a status badge.
  • Deep-linkable, shareable detail pages per unit (full specs, photos, description) with status (Available / Pending / Sold).
  • Lead capture: "Request a quote" and "Sell your trailer" forms create a Lead in the tenant's back office and notify staff; financing pre-qualification entry point.
  • Informational pages (financing, about, contact) and tenant branding (logo, colors, phone, address).
  • Units automatically appear/disappear as their status changes; optional syndication to external listing channels.
Public storefront — generated from available inventory; forms create leads in the back office.

8 · Cross-Cutting Capabilities

  • Global search across units, leads, customers, deals, invoices and documents, scoped to the tenant and the user's permissions.
  • Notifications (in-app + email/SMS) for new leads, approvals needed, overdue AR, delivery updates and title actions.
  • Activity log / audit trail on every entity: status changes, price changes, discounts, payments, document signatures — who, when, why.
  • Comments & @mentions on units, deals and work orders so roles hand off context.
  • Tasks & follow-ups assignable to users, surfaced on each role's dashboard.
  • Document storage & e-signature for titles, bills of sale, financing and delivery receipts.

13 · Acceptance Criteria

The rebuild is "done" when the following end-to-end scenarios pass for a tenant, with figures reconciling across roles:

  1. Acquire→list. Inventory Manager adds an incoming unit; Service completes intake & recon; Pricing sets list/floor; status flips to available and the unit appears on the storefront and syndication.
  2. Lead→quote. A storefront "Request a quote" creates a Lead in the Lead Desk within seconds, routes to a salesperson, who builds a Quote tied to the unit.
  3. Negotiate→approve. A below-floor offer requires Sales Manager approval, which is logged; the Deal advances to financing.
  4. Finance→title→deliver. Customer applies for financing in the portal and sees status; Office tracks title to "in hand"; Dispatch schedules and tracks delivery to "delivered."
  5. Book. Bookkeeper issues the invoice, records payment, and the sale appears in revenue/gross KPIs that match the Owner dashboard.
  6. Operate (hosted). Super-Admin provisions a new tenant, sees it billed, and watches MRR/usage update.
